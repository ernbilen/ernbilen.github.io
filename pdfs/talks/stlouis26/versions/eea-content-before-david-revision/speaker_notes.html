3. Research question

In chess there is a very concrete choice: when losing to a player from the other side, do you resign or keep playing? The direction is not obvious. 7. Chess data

Time: 1:05 | Elapsed: 4:50

Cue: Use the EEA bullet explanation of the comparison.

Say: For a Ukrainian player, for example, compare games against Russian-flag opponents with games against everyone else. Does that difference change after the invasion? We compare within players, controlling for both ratings and calendar changes within each conflict. The assumption is that, without the war's onset, the gap would have followed a similar path. We cannot rule out every other change in who is playing or who meets whom.

Next: "The first result is less resignation against opposing-country opponents."

Source: David's deck, Estimation: the same player, two kinds of opponent, before and after.

8. Results

Time: 1:40 | Elapsed: 6:30

Cue: A modest average decline; explain points versus percent, then the exception.

Say: Players became less likely to resign against an opponent displaying the other side's flag, relative to the comparison games. When each player gets equal weight, the pooled estimate is a decline of 1.46 percentage points. That is about 7 percent of the pre-war resignation rate, not seven percentage points. David's illustration makes the scale concrete: out of 1,000 such games before the war, about 460 were lost and 215 ended in resignation. The estimate amounts to roughly 15 fewer resignations, or about three fewer per 100 lost games using that pre-war loss rate. This is an illustration of the adjusted estimate, not a directly estimated effect conditional on losing. Weighting each game equally gives a smaller estimate, minus 0.46 points. The results also differ across conflicts: Ukraine–Russia and Israel–Palestine have negative estimates; Armenia–Azerbaijan has no detectable resignation change. The raw-record Israel–Palestine estimate is borderline at conventional significance levels (p = .053).

Next: "If fewer games end in resignation, how do they end instead?"

Source: David's deck, headline and scale frames; scale_lost_games.pdf; By conflict. Equal-player pooled estimate: SE 0.64 pp, p = .022. Equal-game estimate: SE 0.13 pp, p = .0005. See backup.html for weighting and conflict estimates.

9. Results

Time: 1:05 | Elapsed: 7:35

Cue: The route to the end changes.

Say: In a separate game-level analysis, counting each game once, resignation endings fall by 1.50 points. Checkmates rise by 0.65 and losses on time by 0.80. Those increases nearly offset the decline in resignation. We do not detect a change in whether games are decisive or drawn. This is consistent with some games that would have ended in resignation continuing until mate or until a clock runs out. We do not observe the counterfactual ending of any individual game. The coefficients here come from a different analysis from the previous slide; their similarity does not make the samples or estimands identical.

Next: "Next, we use the engine to measure continuation in lost positions."

Source: David's deck, Where the missing concessions went: checkmate, or the clock; endings.pdf. The figure uses 1,747,107 physical games, each counted once.

10. Chess data

Time: 0:40 | Elapsed: 8:15

Cue: Define the measure on its own slide, before showing the figure.

Say: We call a position lost when the engine evaluation first reaches minus three pawns or worse for that player. This is a measurement threshold, not a claim that a human game becomes unwinnable. Every game reaching that threshold enters the analysis, regardless of the final result. We then count how long the player continues.

Next: "Here is the change in continuation after reaching that threshold."

Source: David's deck, Measuring continuation after a position is lost.

11. Results

Time: 1:10 | Elapsed: 9:25

Cue: Engine-defined threshold; small average effect; avoid overstating what "lost" means.

Say: Engine analysis lets us identify when a player first reaches an evaluation of minus three pawns or worse. We call that a lost position for this analysis; it does not mean a human game is literally unwinnable. We include every game that reaches the threshold, regardless of how it eventually ends. Against opposing-country opponents after onset, players continued about 0.29 extra half-moves on average: a small, 1.7 percent increase. They were also more likely to continue beyond six half-moves. A half-move is one move by one player. We do not detect more games entering the lost-position group, or a shift in the evaluation at which players resign. This supports a continuation response, while the size remains modest.

Next: "Is that extra continuation accompanied by worse chess?"

Source: David's deck, Measuring continuation after a position is lost; persistence and checks frames; persistence_v3.pdf. Risk set: 1,115,113 player-game observations, 35,925 players. Extra play: +0.288 half-moves, SE 0.061, p < .0001. Beyond six half-moves: +0.63 pp, p = .0011.

12. Results

Time: 0:35 | Elapsed: 10:00

Cue: A small improvement in measured accuracy, not a big practical change.

Say: Average engine-evaluated error actually falls slightly, by 0.229 centipawns per move. It is a small change. Performance relative to ratings does not detectably change. So the extra continuation is not accompanied by deteriorating play.

Next: "We also see a change in opening choices."

Source: David's deck, Move quality; quality_main.pdf. Error SE 0.104, p = .027. Rating-adjusted performance p = .83.

13. Results

Time: 0:40 | Elapsed: 10:40

Cue: Less familiar openings; this does not establish more aggressive openings.

Say: Players departed more from their own pre-war repertoires. The largest change is in Armenia–Azerbaijan, where there is no detectable resignation change. This index measures how familiar an opening is for that player. It does not measure aggressiveness, and we should not claim a clear increase in aggressive openings.

Next: "So, what do we take from these results?"

Source: David's deck, A different margin: departures from habitual openings; openings_main.pdf. Pooled index +0.087, p < .0001.

14. Discussion

Time: 1:00 | Elapsed: 11:40

Cue: Summarize the findings, their size, and the open interpretation.

Say: The broader point is that war can carry over into everyday encounters far away from the fighting. Players became less willing to resign to opponents displaying the other side's flag, and continued a little longer in engine-defined lost positions. Effects are modest and differ across conflicts. Pride, higher stakes, or withholding an acknowledgment of victory could all fit. We observe behavior, not motives. Flags need not be citizenship, and we cannot see games aborted before the first move.

Next: "Thank you. I would welcome your thoughts on how chess players interpret resignation."

Source: David's deck, Limitations; What does it mean to keep playing a lost position?; Conclusion.
